Data Science Preparation FAQ's 3

Created on 2019-03-29 04:12

Published on 2019-03-29 04:17

      “I am a self-learner. I am currently doing x course from y academy/website. Is this the right course? I am having doubts in course and I really need someone to answer questions.”

 

Self-learning is a challenge in itself and in a versatile field like DS, it’s even more challenging. Here are the problems, you will face.

1.     Tremendous amount of information/course/blogs/books/videos

2.     Once you figure out a resource, do we have to complete entire content. Depth first or breadth first approach needed ?

3.     Next, how to get doubts clarification as most academies offer limited support. ?

4.     I understood the concept. But where can I apply and how ?

Tremendous amount of information/course/blogs/books/videos

     The first problem is abundant information online aka information overload. Tons of blogs and academies are thriving on Data Science boom. So how to decide upon a course will be a very difficult task. I will give a few pointers to decide.

·       Have a look at exhaustiveness of curriculum. Generally, any Data Science course must have Descriptive, Inferential statistics, Machine Learning, Deep Learning and NLP. Optimisation is optional as course component.

·       The above words give you understanding of breadth. But even depth should be considered. Most of institutes give less depth at a lesser price. It seems affordable but in fact you are losing money as your time is lost.

·       For a few people, who prefer purchasing text books and going in depth, the course work of those website will be a kick start after which they can read text books. To understand depth of a course – you can take popular textbooks in respective area and compare detailed topics. Number of hours also gives a glimpse. For example, all major topics of ML can be covered in 40-60 hours which indicates it’s a good depth course.

 

Once you figure out course/textbook do we have to complete entire content. Depth first or breadth first approach ?

 

Obviously, the answer is breadth first if you are newbie. Once your complete breadth course, you will know only terminology. But with ‘terminology’ of all concepts which you get with breadth courses, you can’t secure a job. Now the task is to achieve depth. It’s hard to find courses to acquire. It’s all about practise. So, look up at courses which pushes you to practise more.

Next, how to get doubts clarification as most academies / websites offer limited support. ?

 

 For all your doubts, having a mentor who has travelled this path will be very beneficial. Build good LinkedIn network and comment/ write on influencer roles. They will for sure help. Search Linkedin with ‘Data Scientist’ and follow/connect with them. I did this 3 years ago when the crowd is too low. Today, if you do, please be prepared for noise.

 Also, most of the courses offer mentorship in one or other form. If your doubts are coding related, stack overflow has a data science vertical, you can ask there and learn to not depend on anyone for coding doubts. Debug until the bug breaks. Spend time. Its worth it. In this context, unsupervised learning is much better

 Even git could help you sometimes. Pact publications have a lot of freelancer authors who will help you too. You can ask on Quora. Reach out to conferences or webinars and talk to Data Scientists. And finally, you can email me as well and wait for two hours, if you are girl and guys I know you are patient enough to wait for two days. Just kidding !! :P

 

Next, how to get questions answered for doubts. ?

·        Technical doubts : post on stack over flow or any other blogs or slack

·        Approach based questions : post on LinkedIn or Kaggle kernels or slack

·        Career Advice : LinkedIn or Quora

·        For any or all doubts : ask a mentor

Understood the concept. But where can I apply and how ?

·       As I always say, one step at a time. Take Kaggle competitions and practise. You can raise questions in kernels comment sections. Once you kick off, increase complexity of problems

·       Some interesting use cases are put up by people on LinkedIn on their profile. Seek the data and proceed.

·       Keep attending conferences/webinars where speakers talk about latest work and get data for your new problems. Influencers may not listen to your problems but if you ask questions about their work – they will respond for sure. So, this is an ice breaker. Please ask questions and write thank you email or message.

·       Approach start-ups offering work for free and get exposure.

·       And become a voracious reader of textbooks

·       Keep sharing the knowledge because it is volatile


I got a problem application and I applied it. How do I know whether it is correct or not ?

·       Again, another subjective question. Choose the right metric as per business may be MAPE or recall. Then compare with previous result state of art and you get an idea

·       Share results with people and then they justify it. Or talk to a potential customer for this product and take his views.

 

 

I successfully built a model but is this the best.? How to reach best ? And how do I get recognized for this ?

·       There are no best models. 99% accurate models can also be scrapped. That’s the reason companies need a Data Scientist to figure out simple model vs best model incorporating business requirements.

·       To reach best, come up with new ideas, optimise the code, improve domain specific business knowledge etc.

·       Recognition for you – put it on github with a very neat readme.md explaining your approach. Many recruiters check github profile before taking a call

·       Publish on LinkedIn and take their views. Develop a group of senior Data Scientists connections and ask them to review as well

·       Finally, you can order a pizza for me and I can review it for you :P

 

What should I put on resume ? Should I cook up ? What happens if I cook up ?

·       Cooking up projects on resume is dangerous as recruiters are well trained to know about your cooking skills. One advice from me is – you can say you cooked Maggie even though you didn’t. But don’t say you made a biryani without even knowing all the ingredients

 

Difficulties in cracking interviews, not getting calls because of certification etc.

·       If you are getting interview calls, that is good sign. It means you will get a job soon. Analyse what went wrong –  weak basics, not from IT back ground , not a right fit. And come back and work on them

·       If you are not getting calls, then build a brand and profile. Get exposed, practise and network. And you can wade through the storm and rest all is sunshine
